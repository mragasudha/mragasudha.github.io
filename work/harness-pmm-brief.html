Customer need — how I framed the problem

The brief opens with the market problem, not the product. Cloud adoption has outpaced governance — as organizations grow and cloud provisioning decentralizes across teams, cost overruns, compliance gaps, and security risks compound quickly.

Framing choice

I led with the organizational pressure — economic slowdown forcing cost cuts — rather than just the technical challenge. This frames the problem as a C-suite conversation, not just a DevOps one, which matters because the buying decision for a governance tool typically involves both engineering leadership and finance.

Two distinct personas, two different conversations

One of the most common PMM mistakes with a product like this is writing one generic message for "cloud teams." I identified two distinct buyer personas with meaningfully different jobs-to-be-done, pain points, and success metrics — which then drove separate messaging tracks.

Competitive landscape — what I found

The cloud governance space is highly fragmented — around 170 vendors offer some form of cloud management capability. My analysis surfaced the key structural dynamics driving differentiation:

  • Cloud providers (AWS, GCP, Azure) offer native governance tools but are siloed to their own environments — a real gap for multi-cloud organizations
  • Multi-cloud support is the primary differentiator that separates serious platform players (VMware, Turbot) from point solutions
  • AI is a real differentiator — but many vendors were using the term for legacy rule-based logic, not genuine AI capability. This was a signal to be precise about what Harness AIDA actually does
  • Developer experience matters — tools that slow down engineering velocity lose. Speed of governance implementation is a selling point, not just governance quality

Competitive insight that shaped positioning

The genuine AI capability of Harness AIDA — natural language policy creation, automatic rule explanation — was a credible differentiator in a market where most "AI" claims were hollow. The positioning leaned into this specifically rather than using generic AI language.

Positioning and segmented messaging

The positioning statement I developed for Harness Cloud Asset Governance:

"Harness Asset Governance empowers FinOps and DevOps teams to streamline cloud resource governance, security and cost optimization through automation. Instantly create complex, error-free policies using Harness AIDA ensuring seamless resource provisioning and configuration."

From the single positioning statement, I developed two distinct messaging tracks for the primary buyer personas:

For CloudOps / FinOps

"Take control of your cloud costs. Easily track, analyze, and optimize spending, ensuring every cloud dollar is spent wisely. Our software grows with your organization, adapting to changing workloads and resource demands."

For DevOps / Engineering

"Automate governance tasks so you can focus on pipelines without the hassle of manual resource management. Create error-free policies and ensure cloud resources are provisioned correctly with AIDA."

GTM plan

The GTM plan covers pre-launch, launch, and ongoing content marketing activities — each with audience, channel, and timing mapped out. I structured it in two phases to separate the internal readiness work (sales and partner enablement) from the external launch motion.

Pre-launch (1 month before go-live)

  • Internal sales enablement session via webinar — equipping the sales org before external noise
  • Partner-facing enablement materials prepared offline

Launch activities

  • Website update targeting CloudOps, FinOps, DevOps, and executive audiences
  • Press release distributed via PR and amplified on social
  • LinkedIn campaign — biweekly posts linking to PR, website, and content assets
  • Analyst briefings post-launch — allowing the market story to land first before seeking coverage

Content marketing (ongoing)

  • Blog introducing category benefits and Harness positioning (see blog section below — I wrote this)
  • E-guide: "Getting started with asset governance — how to choose a vendor?"
  • Hype video summarizing the offering for social and web
  • Demo videos per use case for YouTube and website
  • Webinar covering pain points, benefits, and live demo
  • Analyst-authored reports (TEI, case studies) for enterprise credibility

The brief also included a community engagement strategy covering content cadence, user feedback loops, event invitations, and a gamification layer to drive active participation — recognizing that Harness had a strong developer community that could be a distribution channel for the launch.

The blog — from strategy to execution

The second deliverable was a finished blog post written to support the GTM plan above. The same positioning and audience understanding that went into the brief directly shaped how the blog was written.

The blog follows the same category-first structure I used in the HCL OneTest e-guide: establish the market problem (cloud governance challenges), introduce the category (Cloud Asset Governance), then position Harness within it — ending with the AIDA differentiator as the specific hook.
