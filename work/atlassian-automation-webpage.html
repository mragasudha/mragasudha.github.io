What I did

The revamp was scoped to three things: messaging and positioning, cross-channel consistency, and the customer logo section. It wasn't a full redesign — the structure and layout stayed intact. The work was about making sure the words and proof points on the page were doing their job as effectively as possible ahead of a high-visibility moment.

Messaging and positioning update. The page headline and key value propositions were refreshed to reflect how we were talking about Automation at that point in time — sharper language around what the product actually does for teams, aligned to the themes we were leading with at Team '23. The core messaging pillars on the live page — removing bottlenecks, scaling operations, enhancing productivity — reflect the positioning framework I worked within.

Cross-channel consistency. The updated messaging wasn't just for the webpage. Part of the work involved making sure the same themes were reflected consistently across related channels — blogs, social, and community forums — so that anyone encountering Atlassian Automation in different places got a coherent story. Inconsistent messaging across channels is a common PMM failure mode, and this was a deliberate effort to close that gap ahead of the event.

Customer logo refresh. Updated the social proof section with recently acquired customer logos — companies like Canva, Dish Wireless, Instacart, Royal Caribbean, and Ferrari — to reflect the actual breadth of the customer base and strengthen the enterprise credibility signal on the page.

Why timing mattered

Team is Atlassian's biggest annual event — the moment when the most eyes are on the product and the brand. Making sure the Automation webpage was sharp and consistent before that traffic spike was the whole point of the timing. A revamp after the event would have missed the window.

The messaging on the page

The headline — "Gain a competitive edge with powerful automation" — positions Automation as a strategic investment, not just a productivity tool. The sub-headline — "Replace manual, time-consuming work with modern processes and workflows" — makes the value concrete and immediate. The three pillars (remove bottlenecks, scale operations, enhance productivity) map cleanly to the different reasons teams adopt automation: speed, growth, and efficiency.
