AI Workflow · Competitive Intelligence

Competitive intelligence in 2 hours, not 2 days

⏱ 2–3 hours end-to-end

Competitive analysis used to take me the better part of a week — reading product pages, sitting through demos, combing through G2, synthesizing notes. This workflow compresses that into a focused afternoon session using three tools in sequence. The AI handles the scanning and first-pass synthesis. I handle the strategic interpretation and so-what.

Perplexity AI Claude Notion AI

The workflow

Step 1 · ~30 min

Market scanning with Perplexity

I use Perplexity (not Google) for the initial scan because it synthesizes live sources with citations. I run 3–5 targeted queries per competitor to build a raw intelligence file.

Prompts I use in Perplexity:

Prompt 1 — positioning scan

What is [Competitor]'s current positioning and messaging for their [product category] product? How have they described it in recent press releases, blog posts, or product pages in the last 6 months?

Prompt 2 — customer sentiment

What are the most common complaints and praises about [Competitor]'s [product] on G2, Reddit, and Hacker News? Focus on what users say about [specific feature area].

Prompt 3 — recent moves

What product launches, pricing changes, or marketing campaigns has [Competitor] made in the last 3 months? Include analyst commentary if available.

Step 2 · ~45 min

Synthesis and gap analysis with Claude

I paste my Perplexity outputs into Claude with a structured prompt that asks it to find patterns, flag positioning gaps, and identify where my product has a credible advantage. This is where the real leverage is.

Claude synthesis prompt

Here is raw competitive intelligence I've gathered on [Competitor A], [Competitor B], and [Competitor C] in the [category] space. [paste Perplexity outputs] My product is [brief description]. Our ICP is [description]. Our core differentiation claim is [claim]. Please analyze this intelligence and produce: 1. A positioning map — how each competitor is positioning relative to each other 2. Messaging gaps — things none of them are saying that could be a whitespace opportunity 3. Where our differentiation claim is credible vs. where it's vulnerable 4. 3 recommended messaging angles we could test, ranked by credibility Be direct. Flag where the data is thin and I need more research.

Sample output (illustrative):

Claude's response — excerpt

Positioning map: Competitor A is anchored on "enterprise-grade reliability," B on "developer-first simplicity," and C on "AI-native automation." None of them are leading with time-to-value or onboarding speed.

Messaging gap: All three assume the buyer already knows why they need this category. None are writing for the "automation-curious but not yet committed" buyer who needs permission to start small. This is a credible whitespace.

Your differentiation — vulnerable here: Your claim of "enterprise-grade" is at risk because Competitor A has 3 Gartner mentions in the last 6 months. Don't lead with that claim without a proof point.

Step 3 · ~30 min

Brief creation with Notion AI

I take the Claude output into Notion and use Notion AI to help format it into a shareable intelligence brief with a one-page executive summary, full analysis, and recommended actions. I then review and edit — the strategic layer and "so what" always needs a human PMM.

What I contribute that AI can't

The workflow above handles research velocity and pattern recognition. What it can't do: assess whether a competitor's move is a real threat or a marketing stunt, know which sales objections are actually coming up in deals, or make the judgment call on which positioning angle to bet on. That's the PMM's job — and it's where experience with technical buyers pays off.

Tools and cost

Perplexity Pro ($20/mo), Claude Pro ($20/mo), Notion (free tier works). Total: $40/mo for a workflow that replaces multiple days of manual research.
