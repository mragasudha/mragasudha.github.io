AI Workflow · Positioning

Drafting positioning documents 3x faster

⏱ 2–4 hours end-to-end

Use this workflow to go from raw research inputs to a structured positioning document in a focused half-day session. Start with your ICP, pain points, competitive landscape, and win/loss themes. Claude handles the first draft — you refine and take it to stakeholders.

Claude Notion

Step 1 · ~30 min

Assemble your inputs before opening Claude

Gather: ICP description, customer pain points, current product description, 2–3 competitor positioning snapshots, your strongest differentiators, and where you typically lose deals.

Step 2 · ~20 min

Generate 3 positioning options

Prompt

I'm a product marketing manager working on positioning for [product name]. ICP: [role, company size, industry] Core pain points: [list 3-5] How we currently describe the product: [current tagline or description] Competitors and how they position: [2-3 competitors] Our strongest differentiators: [list 2-3] Where we lose deals: [list 1-2] Generate 3 distinct positioning options. For each: - Positioning statement (who it's for, what it does, why it's different) - One-line tagline - Why this would resonate with the ICP - What it de-emphasizes or risks Be direct. Flag anything that seems contradictory or weak.

Sample output:

Claude's response — excerpt

Option 1 — Lead with speed-to-value
Positioning: For DevOps teams drowning in manual governance tasks, [Product] is the only platform that enforces complex cloud policies automatically — so engineers can focus on shipping, not configuration.
Tagline: "Govern without slowing down."
Why it resonates: Win/loss data shows you beat competitors when buyers prioritize developer experience.
Risk: De-emphasizes compliance depth — may not land with heavily regulated industries.

Note: Your "strongest differentiator" and "current description" are pointing in different directions. Worth resolving before finalizing.

Step 3 · ~20 min

Stress-test your preferred option

Prompt

I've chosen this positioning for [product]: [paste chosen positioning statement] Stress-test it: 1. What are the strongest reasons this could fail or fall flat? 2. How would a skeptical sales rep push back in a customer conversation? 3. Which competitor is best positioned to attack this, and how? 4. What proof points do I need to make this credible — and do I have them?

Step 4 · ~30 min

Expand into a full positioning document

Prompt

Based on this positioning statement: [paste it] Proof points available: [list them] Draft a positioning document with: 1. Refined positioning statement 2. Three messaging pillars — each with a one-line claim and 2-3 proof points 3. How the positioning angles differently for [Persona A] vs [Persona B] 4. Three messages to retire Format cleanly — I'll paste into Notion and edit from there.
