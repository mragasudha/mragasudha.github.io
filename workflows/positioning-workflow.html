AI Workflow · Positioning

Drafting positioning documents 3x faster

⏱ 2–4 hours end-to-end

Positioning is the hardest PMM deliverable to get right — and historically one of the slowest to produce. The research, synthesis, stakeholder input, and iteration cycles can stretch a positioning project over weeks. This workflow compresses the drafting phase dramatically, freeing up more time for the part that actually matters: refining and stress-testing the positioning with stakeholders.

Claude Notion

What this workflow does — and doesn't do

AI can accelerate the drafting and structuring of a positioning document. It cannot replace the inputs that make positioning credible: customer interviews, win/loss analysis, sales conversations, and a deep understanding of how competitors are actually perceived in the market. This workflow assumes you've done that research. If you haven't, start there.

What changes with AI: instead of staring at a blank doc trying to synthesize everything into coherent positioning, you feed your research into Claude and get a structured first draft in minutes — one you can then push back on, refine, and take to stakeholders.

Step 1 · ~30 min

Assemble your raw inputs

Before opening Claude, pull together everything you know: ICP description, key customer pain points (from interviews or sales calls), how you currently describe the product, competitive positioning you've observed, and any win/loss themes. The quality of your output is only as good as the quality of your inputs — don't skip this.

The prompts

Prompt 1 — Generate positioning options

Claude prompt — positioning options

I'm a product marketing manager working on positioning for [product name]. Here's what I know: ICP: [describe your ideal customer profile — role, company size, industry] Their core pain points: [list 3-5 pain points from customer research] How we currently describe the product: [paste your current tagline or description] Key competitors and how they position: [brief description of 2-3 competitors] Our strongest differentiators (what we hear from customers who choose us): [list 2-3] Where we lose deals (honest): [list 1-2 weaknesses or gaps] Please generate 3 distinct positioning options. For each one give me: - A positioning statement (who it's for, what it does, why it's different) - A one-line tagline - The core narrative logic — why this positioning would resonate with the ICP - What it de-emphasizes or risks Be direct. Flag if any of the inputs seem contradictory or weak.

Sample output (illustrative):

Claude's response — excerpt

Option 1 — Lead with speed-to-value
Positioning: For DevOps teams drowning in manual governance tasks, [Product] is the only platform that enforces complex cloud policies automatically — so engineers can focus on shipping, not configuration.
Tagline: "Govern without slowing down."
Logic: Your win/loss data shows you beat competitors when buyers prioritize developer experience. This positioning doubles down on that.
Risk: De-emphasizes compliance depth — may not land with heavily regulated industries.

Note: Your "strongest differentiator" and "how we currently describe the product" are pointing in different directions — customers value the automation, but your current description leads with cost savings. Worth resolving before finalizing.

Why 3 options matter

Presenting one positioning statement to stakeholders invites editing. Presenting three invites a strategic conversation about which direction to bet on. The options also help surface internal disagreements early — which is valuable information.

Step 2 · ~20 min

Stress-test your preferred option

Once you've picked the direction that feels strongest, run it through a second prompt to pressure-test it before taking it to stakeholders.

Claude prompt — stress test

I've chosen this positioning direction for [product]: [paste your chosen positioning statement] Please stress-test it by: 1. Arguing against it — what are the strongest reasons this positioning could fail or fall flat? 2. How would a skeptical sales rep push back on this in a customer conversation? 3. Which competitor is best positioned to attack this, and how? 4. What proof points would I need to make this credible — and do I have them? Be harsh. I'd rather find the holes now than in a customer meeting.

Step 3 · ~30 min

Build the full positioning document in Notion

Take your chosen positioning and stress-test feedback into Notion. Use Claude one more time to expand the positioning statement into a full document — with messaging pillars, proof points per pillar, and persona-specific angles. Then review and edit: the strategic layer, the judgment calls, and the internal framing still need a human PMM.

Claude prompt — full document

Based on this positioning statement: [paste it] And these proof points I have available: [list them] Please draft a positioning document with: 1. Positioning statement (refined) 2. Three messaging pillars — each with a one-line claim and 2-3 supporting proof points 3. How the positioning angles differently for [Persona A] vs [Persona B] 4. Three things this positioning means we should stop saying (messaging to retire) Format it cleanly — I'll paste this into Notion and edit from there.

What I contribute that AI can't

The workflow produces a strong draft fast. What it can't do: know which proof points will actually land in your specific market, judge whether a positioning claim is credible given your brand equity, or navigate the internal politics of getting stakeholders aligned. That's the PMM's job — and where the real work happens after the draft exists.

Time saved

A positioning document that used to take me 2–3 days of drafting and iteration now takes a focused half-day session. The time savings go back into more stakeholder conversations and better research — which is where positioning actually gets good.
